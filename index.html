What already works

  • You show up every week. 89 posts in 12 months, never more than 8 days between two posts.
  • People can find you on the map. 63 of those 89 posts are tagged with your location.
  • You have the material. Your rooms, the arch on the gift cards, fire cupping, HydraScalp, the fountain by your entrance. It’s already in your feed, just buried between memes.
  • A warm, local voice. “Happy Halloween, Columbus!”, the Pride special, the Thanksgiving food drive.
  • Your website does the hard part. Prices, packages, last-minute openings and online booking up to two hours before start time. Instagram only has to lead people there.

  • Bookings and calls that came from Instagram. A “How did you hear about us?” question with an Instagram option at booking or check-in. Your job application form already asks it.
  • Taps on the booking link in your bio, from Instagram Insights, week by week.

What to expect in 1–3 months

Month 1The page answers the first questions on its own: how to book, where you are, what to choose, how to give a gift card. The first reels with your own footage go out.
Month 2I look at which topics reach people who don’t follow you yet and which bring messages and taps on the booking link, and write the next scripts around them.
Month 3A steady pace and your own numbers to compare against. Reels that did well on their own can be boosted.
These are expectations, not promises. Instagram ranks reels by how long people watch, how many like them and how many send them to someone (Instagram), and it shows reposted content to fewer new people (Instagram for Creators). That is why the plan leans on your own footage. The result depends on getting the shoot done in the first two weeks, someone answering messages during opening hours, and the holiday season. I’ll measure everything from your starting point this month.
